# Productive Thinking Model

Also: PTM. A six-step problem-solving process by Tim Hurson, from his book Think Better. Use it for a problem with large consequences.

## Structure

Six steps. Use these names in this order.

| Step | Tool or output |
|---|---|
| 1. What's Going On? | Target Future: the vision |
| 2. What's Success? | DRIVE: Do (expected outcome), Restrictions (what to avoid), Investment (resources), Values, Essential outcomes |
| 3. What's the Question? | Catalytic question: answer it to reach the Target Future |
| 4. Generate Answers | Third third: the last ideas, after the obvious ones |
| 5. Forge the Solution | POWER: Positives, Objections, What else?, Enhancements, Remedies |
| 6. Align Resources | Implementation plan |

The model separates creative from critical thinking: first make a long list without judgment, then choose.

## Steps

1. Answer five questions: What is the problem? What is its impact? What information do you have? Who is involved? What is the vision of the future when the problem is solved? The vision is the Target Future
2. Write the success criteria with DRIVE
3. List questions in the form "How might we ...?" Choose one: the catalytic question
4. List answers to it until you reach the third third
5. Choose the best answers. Refine them with POWER
6. Plan the implementation

## Checks

- Wrong author or names: the author is Tim Hurson. Do not change the step names or the DRIVE and POWER words
- Judgment during a list: complete the list, then choose
- Early stop: the obvious answers come first, the most valuable ones last
- Lists made in a group: studies of brainstorming (Diehl and Stroebe, 1987) found that persons who work alone produce more ideas than a group. Let each person list alone first

## Wrong Tool When

- A small tactical decision: six steps are too heavy
- Open exploration: the steps are too rigid

## Sources

- Hurson, Tim. Think Better: An Innovator's Guide to Productive Thinking. McGraw-Hill, 2007
